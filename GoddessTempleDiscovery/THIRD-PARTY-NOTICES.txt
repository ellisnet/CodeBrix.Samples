THIRD-PARTY NOTICES
===================

This file documents third-party content that is bundled with, or used at run
time by, the Goddess Temple Discovery sample application, together with the
licenses under which that content is provided.

Third-party CODE dependencies are consumed as NuGet packages. Each package
carries its own license and third-party notices in its own repository/package
(the CodeBrix.* packages ship their own THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt), so those are
not reproduced here. That includes the game engine and its CardsAndDice
add-on, the AppSettings add-in and the PDF document library the Field Journal
is drawn with.

Note that the CodeBrix packages this application uses are not all under the
same license; the family's package-name suffix records each one's license, so
check the suffix in each csproj before shipping a derivative.

Nothing is downloaded at run time. Everything the application shows travels
inside its assemblies or inside the packages it references.

Licensed under the SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1.
Full license text:
  src/libs/GoddessTempleDiscovery.Assets/Fonts/OFL-Merriweather.txt

Three faces (Regular, Bold, Italic) are embedded, unmodified, as resources in
the GoddessTempleDiscovery.Assets assembly, with the license text embedded
beside them. They set the titles and text of the card faces, the HUD and the
Field Journal. On the card faces the letters are drawn as glyph outlines
(SVG path data) generated from these faces at run time; in the Field Journal
the faces are registered with the PDF font system and subsets of them are
embedded in every PDF the application writes.

The same typeface also reaches the application's XAML text through the
CodeBrix.Platform.Fonts.Merriweather package, where it is set as the
application-wide default text font; that copy ships inside the package rather
than in this folder.

CardsAndDice add-on content  (arrives with its NuGet package, not bundled here)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The card back on every deck (the add-on's celestial back), the traditional
pip dice, the deal, flip and roll animations, and the card and dice sounds
(including the card-slide sound the game reuses as the newspaper's paper
rustle) come from the CardsAndDice add-on of CodeBrix.Platform.GameEngine,
inside its package. The add-on's own THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt is the record for
that content: among other things it credits the public-domain Star of Ishtar
drawing (Raphael 75, Wikimedia Commons) that its celestial back incorporates
inside an original frame, and the Kenney (https://kenney.nl) Casino Audio
sounds it carries under Creative Commons Zero (CC0). Nothing of the add-on's
content is copied into this folder.

Original vector art
  (bundled: src/libs/GoddessTempleDiscovery.Assets/Art/<category>/*.svg)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every picture in the game - the buildings and their plans, the objects, the
symbols and icons, the deities and heroes, the scenes, the traced plates, the
chrome and the Art Deco pieces - is original vector work made for this
application. It is licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0, the license

The pictures are drawn from an understanding of the published excavation
reports, plans and museum objects; no existing image is copied, embedded or
converted into them, except as follows for the traced plates.

Traced plates  (bundled: src/libs/GoddessTempleDiscovery.Assets/Art/plates/*.svg)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plates are vector tracings - posterized to the art's palette and redrawn
as paths - of public-domain and CC0 photographs and engravings. No photograph
or engraving is itself included. Each plate's header comment names its source;
the sources are:

  - plate-cone-mosaic-columns (Cone-Mosaic Half-Columns): photograph by
    Neoclassicism Enthusiast, Wikimedia Commons, 2023, CC0 1.0.
  - plate-cone-mosaic-niche (Cone-Mosaic Niche, the Pillar Terrace wall,
    VA 10997): photograph by Neoclassicism Enthusiast, Wikimedia Commons, CC0.
  - plate-karaindash-facade (The Karaindash Temple Facade, VA 10983):
    photograph by Neoclassicism Enthusiast, Wikimedia Commons, CC0.
  - plate-jordan-eanna-mound-from-above (The Eanna Mound from Above, 1930):
    after Julius Jordan, UVB II (1930) plate 6, Internet Archive scan; public
    domain (Jordan died in 1945).
  - plate-julius-jordan-1900 (Julius Jordan, 1900): studio photograph by
    Hahn's Nachf., Dresden, 1900, via Wikimedia Commons; public domain by age.
  - plate-loftus-coffin-bearers-1857 (Coffin Bearers at Warka, 1857):
    illustration from W. K. Loftus, Travels and Researches in Chaldaea and
    Susiana (London 1857), Internet Archive scan; public domain.
  - plate-schwarzenbach-dig-workers (Dig Workers at Warka, 1934),
    plate-schwarzenbach-jordan-with-the-lion (Julius Jordan with the Lion of
    Babylon, 1934), plate-schwarzenbach-ziggurat-1934 (The Ziggurat of

Oracc: AMGG, the Oracc Sign List and DCCLT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Facts on the Pantheon and Goddess Tablets rest in part on the pages of Ancient

Sign readings rest in part on the Oracc Sign List (http://oracc.org/osl/),
which is CC0 (public domain).

DCCLT (Oracc), 2019
(http://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/lexicallistsperiods/archaic/), with that
attribution on the card.

Museum catalogues, Oracc SelBI, CDLI and the DAI Uruk Visualisation
Project
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Many Discovery cards take their facts from online records, each cited in the
card's sources with its URL and the date it was read:

  - museum-digital SMB (https://smb.museum-digital.de/), the online
    catalogue of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin: the object records of the
    Vorderasiatisches Museum (VA numbers). The cards describe the objects in
    their own words from these records.
  - Oracc CAMS/SelBI (https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/cams/selbi/): the
    Anu-uballiṭ inscriptions. The cards quote a few short phrases of the
    project's English translations, attributed to Oracc on the card.
  - CDLI, the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative (https://cdli.earth/):
    artifact records, with their transliterations. The cards quote a few
    transliterated lines, attributed to CDLI on the card.
  - The Uruk Visualisation Project of the German Archaeological Institute
    (DAI), on the Artefacts Berlin site
    (https://www.artefacts-berlin.de/?p=1494): facts on the holy buildings
    of Seleucid Uruk.

A few cards also cite the British Museum's collection online, CORE and ISAC
News & Notes, read through search summaries only.

The cards' sources record no terms of use for these sites; the terms each site
states apply to the material on it. The quoted phrases and lines are a few
words or lines each, attributed on the card that carries them, and are NOT
relicensed under the Apache License with the rest of this application.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
Other quotations and the card texts
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The other quotations - the excavators' own words from their preliminary
reports, the inscriptions of the kings who built in Eanna, and scholars'
sentences from the published literature, among them Annette Zgoll's English
of Enheduanna's hymn in Uruk: First City of the Ancient World (2019) - are a
sentence or a few lines each, attributed on the card that carries them, with
the publication in its sources. The epigraph on the title screen is Julius
Jordan's passage on the descent of the heavenly powers, from the Dritter
vorläufiger Bericht (Third Preliminary Report), Berlin, 1932, pp. 33–34, in
English.

Every other text in the game - the cards, the headlines, the season stories,
the people, the periods, the glossary, the prologue and epilogue, How to Play
and the credits - is written for this application from the sources each entry
names, and is licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0, with the rest of
the application.
